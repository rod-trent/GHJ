## Theme 9 — My definition of excellent work

> Have a *philosophy* **and** a real example where you hit the bar and knew it. Pick a different
> lens than Theme 4: there, Must Learn KQL was a *decision*; here, make it about a *quality bar*
> and how you *measured* it.

**Draft (spoken):**

"**My definition:** good work is correct; excellent work is correct *and* understood and reusable
by the next person — it solves the real underlying problem, it's maintainable, and you can
actually *measure* that it landed. In my world, the last part matters most: it's easy to produce
content that looks great and teaches nothing. Excellent means the audience can go *do* the thing
afterward.

**An example:** with Must Learn KQL I could see people reading it, but 'read' isn't 'learned.' So
I held myself to a higher bar — I built an actual **assessment** `[VERIFY: Must Learn KQL
Assessment, ~2022]` so learners could *prove* they could write KQL, and so I'd get a real signal
about where the material was failing. That did two things: it turned a pile of articles into
something with a measurable outcome, and the results told me exactly which sections to rewrite
`[VERIFY: what the data showed / pass rates?]`. I iterated on the weak spots instead of guessing.

**Why that's my bar:** understanding the real problem (people needed to *apply* KQL, not admire
it), maintainability (open and versioned so it survives me), communication (small, teachable
pieces), and a measurable outcome (the assessment). When all four line up, I know it's excellent —
not because it's polished, but because it *changed what someone could do*
`[VERIFY: adoption/impact metric?]`."

**Reflection line:** "I stopped trusting 'this looks great' as a quality signal years ago — now I
ask 'how will I know it actually worked?' before I ship, and build that measure in from the start."
