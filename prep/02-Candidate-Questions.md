# Questions to ask them

Goal: **start a conversation**, not fetch a fact you could Google. Pick 4–6 you genuinely
care about; have a couple extra in reserve. Jot a one-line note after each answer — referencing
it later ("you mentioned earlier…") is a strong signal. Tailor each to the interviewer's role
(researcher vs. manager vs. DevRel).

## About the mission & where it's going
1. The Lab's premise is that you can't out-hire the problem — you scale expertise through the
   community. **Where does that model work best today, and where does it still break down?**
2. With the **Taskflow Agent** and AI-assisted triage, how is the Lab thinking about the line
   between what the LLM decides and what a human researcher must still own?
3. If you look 12–18 months out, **where does the Lab most want to have more impact** that it
   can't quite reach yet?

## About research → impact (their phrase)
4. When a researcher finds a novel bug class, what does the path from **"interesting finding"
   to "query shipping in code scanning / MRVA"** actually look like — and where does it get stuck?
5. How do you measure whether a piece of research actually moved the needle for the ecosystem,
   versus just generating a CVE?

## About the Developer Advocate role specifically
6. For this DevRel role, **what does a great first 90 days look like** — more writing/queries,
   more community/conference presence, or more maintainer enablement?
7. How does the Lab decide **which audiences to invest in** — individual maintainers, security
   researchers, or enterprise security teams? They need pretty different stories.
8. What's a piece of security tech the team believes is genuinely important but that developers
   still **don't "get"** — where better advocacy would change adoption?

## About the craft & technical decisions
9. CodeQL constantly trades **precision vs. coverage**. How does the team decide when a query
   is "good enough" to enable by default versus leave opt-in?
10. On the supply-chain work — install scripts off by default, OIDC trusted publishing — **how
    do you weigh ecosystem friction against security** when changing a default that affects
    millions of builds?

## About the team & how it works
11. GitHub is famously remote/async. **How does the Lab do deep security research
    asynchronously** — what's written down vs. what still needs a synchronous room?
12. When the team disagrees on a technical call, how does that usually get resolved?

## Closing-style question (good last one)
13. **What separates someone who's good in this role from someone who's exceptional?**

---
**Avoid:** "What does the Security Lab do?", "Is CodeQL free?", benefits/logistics (save for
recruiter), anything answered on the first page of their site.
