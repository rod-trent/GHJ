# Behavioral & culture workbook

The behavioral interview is **half the loop** and they grade it hard. You can't script it, but
you *should* walk in with ~6–8 real stories you can flex across themes. This workbook gives you
the structure; **you supply the stories.** Paste rough notes back to me and I'll help shape any
of them into tight STAR+R form.

> Format for every answer: **STAR + Reflection**. Spend most of your airtime on
> **Action → Result → Reflection**, not background. Use **"I"**, not "we" — they want *your*
> contribution. ~2 minutes per answer.
>
> S — Situation (1–2 sentences of context)
> T — Task (what *you* were responsible for)
> A — Action (what *you specifically* did, and *why* — reasoning matters)
> R — Result (what changed; quantify if you can)
> **R — Reflection (what you learned and now do differently)**

## Theme 1 — Motivation: why security, why GitHub, why the Security Lab
**They explicitly warn against "GitHub is a great company."** Be specific and personal.
Prompts:
- What's the *origin moment* that pulled you toward security?
- Why **open-source** security specifically — what do you believe about it?
- Why the **Security Lab** over other security teams? (Use the research brief: the "scale
  expertise through community" mission, MRVA, query bounties, Taskflow Agent.)
- This is a **Developer Advocate** role — connect your love of *teaching/communicating* security
  to their mission of enabling the community. Your two talks are living proof.

Draft:
- Why security: _______
- Why open source: _______
- Why the Security Lab (name a specific program + why it resonates): _______
- Why me / why this role (the DevRel + security combination): _______

## Theme 3 — Collaboration & different perspectives
Prompt: a time someone approached a problem differently, you sought the challenge, and your
approach *changed*. Emphasize what changed and what you learned — not that you were right.
- S/T: _______  · A (what *you* did to invite the other view): _______
- R + Reflection: _______

## Theme 4 — Technical problem solving
Use their exact frame: **Problem → Options → Trade-offs → Decision → Outcome → Learning.**
They care about *reasoning* as much as the result. Have at least one meaty one.
- Problem: _______
- Options considered: _______
- Trade-offs weighed: _______
- Decision + why: _______
- Outcome: _______
- Learning: _______

## Theme 5 — Helping & developing others
Prompt: someone you mentored/coached. Strong version = it was **two-way** (you learned too).
- Who / situation: _______
- What you did: _______
- Their outcome + **what you learned from them**: _______

## Theme 6 — Learning & growth
Prompt: what you're *actively* learning and **what you're doing with it** (concrete > general).
- Currently learning: _______
- Applied how: _______

## Theme 7 — Remote & asynchronous collaboration
GitHub is heavily distributed. Prompt: show how you communicate in writing, document, keep
people informed, and **decide when sync is actually needed**.
- Example of great async work (a doc, an RFC, a well-run issue/PR thread): _______
- A time you chose sync *on purpose* and why: _______

## Theme 8 — Ownership & ambiguity
Their desired arc: **recognized the gap → created clarity → took ownership → brought others
along → improved it → applied the learning after.** Avoid blaming others for the ambiguity.
- The gap I noticed: _______
- How I created clarity: _______
- What I owned / how I brought others along: _______
- Improvement + what I carried forward: _______

## Theme 9 — Your definition of excellent work
Have a *philosophy* **and** a real example. Good vs. excellent often = understanding the
underlying problem, maintainability, impact, communication, measurable outcomes.
- My definition (2–3 sentences): _______
- An example where I hit that bar (and how I knew): _______

## Theme 10 — A failure / something you'd do differently
(Not explicitly listed but almost always asked.) Pick a real one; lead with the learning.
- What happened, what I owned, what changed since: _______
